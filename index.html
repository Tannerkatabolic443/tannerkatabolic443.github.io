📡 What This Application Does

Every Wi-Fi network, wireless doorbell, baby monitor, and Bluetooth speaker fills the air around you with invisible radio waves. When too many devices crowd onto the same channel, your internet slows down, video calls freeze, and games lag. The problem is that you normally cannot see any of this happening.

This application makes the invisible visible. Your ESP32-C5 board acts as the ears, listening across the 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz bands using the ESPARGOS and esp-sdr firmware. The software on your Windows computer draws what the board hears as a friendly graph. Tall peaks mean strong signals. Crowded areas look like a busy skyline. Quiet, empty space looks flat.

You do not need to understand radio engineering to use it. If you can plug in a USB cable and click a button, you can run a full wireless survey of your home or office in a few minutes.

Who this is useful for

  • Home users who want to know why their Wi-Fi feels slow in certain rooms
  • People setting up a new router and looking for the least crowded channel
  • Small office and café owners checking whether neighbouring networks interfere
  • Hobbyists and students exploring how wireless signals behave in the real world
  • Anyone curious about how many wireless devices are quietly active around them

✨ Key Features

  • Live 2.4 GHz scanning. Watch every network on the crowded 2.4 GHz band refresh in real time as you move around the room.
  • Live 5 GHz scanning. See the wider, faster 5 GHz band and find out which channels still have breathing room.
  • Clear visual graphs. Signals are drawn as simple peaks and hills, so you can spot a busy channel at a glance without reading numbers.
  • Channel congestion overview. A summary view shows which channels are the busiest and which are the quietest.
  • Signal strength at a glance. Strong nearby transmitters appear tall, distant ones appear small, so you can judge distance and power.
  • Works fully offline. Once the board is connected, everything happens locally on your computer. No account, no cloud, no internet connection required.
  • Small and lightweight. The whole application is a compact download that runs comfortably on older laptops.
  • No coding required. The firmware side is handled by the ESPARGOS and esp-sdr stack, so you never have to open a code editor.

🖥️ What You Need Before You Start

Before downloading, take a minute to check that you have the following items ready. Everything listed is common and inexpensive.

  • A Windows computer. Windows 10 or Windows 11, 64-bit, with at least 4 GB of memory.
  • About 250 MB of free disk space. This covers the application and its temporary scan files.
  • An ESP32-C5 development board. The board must be the C5 model, because the 5 GHz radio is what makes this project possible.
  • A data-capable USB cable. Some cables only carry power. Use one that can transfer data, typically the cable that came with the board.
  • A USB port on your computer. Any standard USB-A or USB-C port works.
  • A USB serial driver. Most boards use the CP210x or CH340 chip. Windows 11 usually installs this automatically the first time you plug the board in.
  • A small antenna. Most ESP32-C5 boards include one. Screwing it in firmly makes a noticeable difference to what you can detect.

You do not need an internet connection after the download is complete, and you do not need to install any extra programming tools.

    🧭 How to Use It

    Once the graph is live, you only need a few simple habits to get useful results.

    Reading the graph

    • The horizontal line across the bottom represents the radio frequencies, running from low on the left to high on the right.
    • The vertical height of each peak shows how strong that signal is. Taller means louder and usually closer.
    • A wide, lumpy hill means several devices are sharing the same space. A sharp, narrow spike is usually a single strong transmitter.
    • Flat, low areas are clear air. Those are the channels where your own router will perform best.

    A simple way to find your best channel

    1. Start the scan and leave it running for about a minute so every device has a chance to appear.
    2. Look along the bottom of the graph for the widest stretch of flat, empty space.
    3. Write down the channel number shown at that position.
    4. Open your router's settings in a web browser and change your Wi-Fi channel to that number.
    5. Restart the scan and compare. The peak for your own network should now sit in the quiet gap.

    Walking around for a room-by-room check

    Because the board is small and battery-free, it is easy to carry on a laptop. Start the scan in one room, then walk slowly to the next. Watch the peaks change as walls and floors get in the way. You will quickly learn which rooms are quiet and which are crowded, which is exactly the information you need when placing a router or a mesh extender.

    🛠️ Troubleshooting

    The application opens but no signals appear

    Check that the USB cable is fully pushed into both ends. Try a different USB port, ideally one on the back of a desktop computer rather than a hub. Confirm the antenna is attached. Then press Scan again.

    Windows does not recognise the board

    Open Device Manager from the Start menu. Look for an entry with a yellow warning triangle, often listed as an unknown device or as a USB serial port. Right-click it and choose Update driver, then let Windows search automatically. On older computers you may need to install the CP210x or CH340 driver from the board maker's website.

    The graph updates very slowly

    Close other programs that use a lot of processing power, especially video editors and games. A slower refresh rate is normal on older laptops and does not stop the scan from working.

    The 5 GHz view looks empty

    5 GHz signals travel a shorter distance and are blocked more easily by walls. Move the board and laptop closer to the router, or into the room you want to check. Also make sure you are viewing the 5 GHz tab rather than the 2.4 GHz tab.

    The peaks look flat and small everywhere

    A loose antenna is the most common cause. Tighten it gently by hand. Metal desks, filing cabinets, and thick walls all weaken signals, so try the scan again from a more open position.

    💡 Practical Tips for Better Results

    • Run a scan at different times of day. Evening is usually the busiest, which is exactly when slow Wi-Fi is most noticeable.
    • Scan before and after moving your router. Small changes in height and position can make a surprising difference.
    • Keep the board away from your laptop's own Wi-Fi antenna if you can. A short USB extension cable helps.
    • Use the 5 GHz view when your devices support it. It is usually far less crowded than 2.4 GHz.
    • Save a screenshot of a quiet scan so you have a baseline to compare against later.
    • Repeat the survey after adding a new smart device, since each one adds a little more traffic to the air.

    ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

    Do I need to know how to program?

    No. The ESPARGOS and esp-sdr firmware is already prepared. You download the application, plug in the board, and press Scan.

    Does this work without an internet connection?

    Yes. The only time you need the internet is to download the application in the first place. After that, everything runs locally.

    Will this interfere with my own Wi-Fi?

    No. The board only listens. It does not transmit, so it cannot disturb your network or anyone else's.

    Can I use a different ESP32 board?

    This project relies on the ESP32-C5 because of its 5 GHz radio. Other ESP32 models do not have the same radio hardware.

    Is my data collected?

    No. Scans stay on your computer. Nothing is uploaded anywhere.

    Can I use it on a Mac or Linux computer?

    The instructions on this page are written for Windows. The project is open source, so people comfortable with other systems can look at the code, but this guide focuses on Windows.
